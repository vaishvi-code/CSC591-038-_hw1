\subsection*{Sparsity Before and After SVD}

Pipeline: (1)~$A$ sparse; (2)~$M$ built as dense $N{\times}N$ float64 (both code paths);
(3)~$Y=\log(\max(M,1))$ stored as sparse CSR at $T{=}1$ (entries $<$1 map to 0), dense at $T{=}10$;
(4)~$N{\times}128$ embedding is 100\% dense. All adjacency matrices are verified symmetric
(\texttt{(A!=A.T).nnz==0}); \texttt{nnz(A)} therefore counts $2|E|$ for all datasets.

  \caption{\small Singular value decay of $Y$ at $T{=}1$. Percentages: retained energy $\sum_{i=1}^{128}\sigma_i^2/\sum_i\sigma_i^2$.
  At $T{=}10$: PPI~94.1\%, BC~98.9\%, Wiki~99.0\%.}
  \label{fig:svd}
\end{figure}

Spectra drop steeply in the first 20--50 ranks, then flatten; $d{=}128$ lies in this flat tail.
At $T{=}1$, 128 singular values retain 63--71\% of squared spectral energy---roughly 30--37\% discarded.
The paper chooses $d{=}128$ for embedding compactness, accepting this truncation cost.
At $T{=}10$ the spectrum concentrates sharply (94--99\% retained), because multi-step random-walk
smoothing transfers spectral mass to top components. This also explains why Theorem~3.5 of~\cite{netmf}
(which bounds the eigenpair approximation error of $D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$, separately from the SVD truncation
of $Y$) gives a tight bound: the discarded eigenvalues of the normalized adjacency are small.

Memory grows exactly as $N^2$: theoretical values (115, 174, 811~MiB local; 48.3~GiB Flickr) match
measured RSS to within noise. Flickr exceeds the 32~GiB local limit; even with in-place operations the
minimum is one copy ($\approx$48~GiB). The naive \texttt{np.log(np.maximum(M,\,1))} creates three copies
($\approx$145~GiB), used on ARC where the 192~GiB node RAM suffices.
Time scaling over local datasets: 2.65$\times$ more nodes (PPI $\to$ BlogCatalog) gives 4.1$\times$
more time at both $T{=}1$ and $T{=}10$ ($\approx N^{1.45}$). The Flickr ARC point ($T{=}1$, 177~s)
is consistent: 7.8$\times$ more nodes $\to$ 18.6$\times$ more time ($\approx N^{1.42}$).
Three points cannot pin down an exponent precisely, but the sub-quadratic trend ($N^{1.4}$--$N^{1.5}$)
is consistent across all four datasets. SVD dominates at $T{=}1$ (e.g., 7.7 of 9.5~s for BlogCatalog);
construction dominates at $T{=}10$ (14.1 of 21.4~s).

\section{Closed-Form Factorization vs.\ Stochastic Training}
%% =========================================================

Theorem~2.3 of~\cite{netmf} proves that DeepWalk implicitly factorizes a closed-form PMI matrix,
providing a rigorous algebraic foundation to what was previously understood only empirically.
Embeddings are deterministic and hyperparameter-free; for $N\lesssim10{,}000$ NetMF achieves
significant gains (up to 50\% relative Micro-F1 over DeepWalk on PPI at 10\% training).

However, both code paths materialise an $N{\times}N$ float64 matrix, imposing an $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$
memory wall that is independent of graph sparsity or window size.
By contrast, DeepWalk and node2vec sample random walks at $\mathcal{O}(|E|)$ cost per epoch and
maintain $\mathcal{O}(Nd)$ memory---orders of magnitude smaller for large $N$.
Stochastic methods are the only viable option for web-scale graphs ($N\sim10^6$--$10^9$),
while NetMF is limited to $N\lesssim15{,}000$ on commodity hardware ($\approx$32~GB RAM).
The key contribution of NetMF is therefore theoretical: grounding stochastic methods algebraically
enables principled improvements such as choosing the factorization target or injecting side information
into $M$, without altering the random-walk procedure.
