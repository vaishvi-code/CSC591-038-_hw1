\section{Baseline Reproduction \& Multi-Label Classification}
%% =========================================================

\textbf{Setup.} We reproduce NetMF~\cite{netmf} on BlogCatalog, PPI, Wikipedia, and Flickr
using one-vs-rest logistic regression (scikit-learn) with 10-fold cross-validation (training ratios 10--90\%).
One fix was required: \texttt{import scipy.sparse.linalg} added to \texttt{netmf.py} (submodule missing on SciPy~1.x).
\textbf{Hardware:} local experiments on a workstation with $\leq$32~GB RAM (Python 3.11, NumPy/SciPy);
Flickr ($N{=}80{,}513$, needs $\approx$52~GB) ran on NC State ARC cluster
(partition \texttt{normal}, 192~GB RAM/node, 4 CPU cores).

\subsection*{Sparsity Before and After SVD Truncation}

\texttt{netmf\_small} ($T{=}1$): forms a dense $N{\times}N$ matrix $M$, applies $Y=\log(\max(M,1))$, stores $Y$ as sparse CSR (entries with $M_{ij}<1$ become $\log(1)=0$). \texttt{netmf\_large} ($T{=}10$): eigenpair approximation avoids $T$ sparse multiplications but still materialises a dense $N{\times}N$ \texttt{mmT} before applying log---\emph{both paths require $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ peak memory}. The final $N{\times}d$ embedding is 100\% dense.

  \caption{\small Singular value decay of $Y$ at $T{=}1$. Percentages: retained energy $\sum_{i=1}^{128}\sigma_i^2/\sum_i\sigma_i^2$.
  At $T{=}10$: PPI~94.1\%, BC~98.9\%, Wiki~99.0\%.}
  \label{fig:svd}
\end{figure}

The spectra drop steeply in the first 20--50 ranks, then flatten; $d{=}128$ lies in this flat tail.
At $T{=}1$, retaining 128 singular values captures 63--71\% of total squared spectral energy---meaning
roughly 30--37\% is discarded, a non-trivial truncation. $d{=}128$ is nonetheless the paper's chosen
dimension and captures the dominant structure while keeping the embedding compact.
At $T{=}10$ the spectrum is far more concentrated (94--99\% retained energy), because multi-step
diffusion smooths spectral mass toward the top components---this also explains why the rank-256
eigenpair approximation of $D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$ provides a good surrogate for $M$ (Theorem~3.5 of~\cite{netmf} bounds this eigenvalue approximation error, separately from the SVD truncation of $Y$).

matrix construction dominates at $T{=}10$ (14.1~s of 21.4~s).

\section{Closed-Form Factorization vs.\ Stochastic Training}
%% =========================================================

\textbf{NetMF strengths.} Theorem~2.3 of~\cite{netmf} proves DeepWalk implicitly factorizes a
closed-form PMI matrix, giving a rigorous algebraic foundation to what was previously empirical.
Embeddings are deterministic, require no SGD hyperparameter tuning, and for small graphs
($N\lesssim10{,}000$) achieve significant gains---up to 50\% relative Micro-F1 improvement over DeepWalk on PPI.

\textbf{Scalability limits.} Closed-form factorization requires $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$ memory regardless
of graph sparsity or window size. DeepWalk and node2vec maintain $\mathcal{O}(Nd)$ memory
and sample walks at $\mathcal{O}(|E|)$ cost per epoch---orders of magnitude more efficient.
For Flickr ($N{=}80{,}513$), NetMF requires 192~GB-class hardware; for web-scale graphs
($N\sim10^6$--$10^9$), only stochastic methods are viable.

\textbf{Accuracy--efficiency trade-off.} NetMF's accuracy advantage comes at a steep memory cost.
The key contribution is theoretical---proving stochastic methods implicitly optimize a well-defined matrix
objective---enabling principled improvements such as choosing the factorization target or incorporating
side information into $M$, without modifying the random-walk machinery.
